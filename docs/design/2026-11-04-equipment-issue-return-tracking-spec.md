# Equipment issue and return tracking

## Problem Statement

Organizations issue laptops, keys, uniforms, and tools to employees but lack a dependable record of who holds them, their condition, and what must be returned. Quantity-based equipment can come back in parts and in different conditions. When someone leaves, outstanding equipment can disappear from current employee lists even though the return obligation remains. A closed exception can also be confused with physical receipt, while an equipment reminder must never keep employment or system access active.

## Solution

Provide organization-scoped equipment accountability for employee-held equipment. Authorized staff register individually identified items or quantities of interchangeable equipment, record each issuance and physical receipt with condition, and distinguish required returns from equipment issued to keep. Employees can see their own retained history. Owners and admins can delegate equipment officer responsibility to the whole organization or selected teams and employees.

Connect outstanding returns to both scheduled departures and immediate deactivation. A departure supplies a return deadline but never waits for the equipment to come back before ending employment or access. Outstanding returns remain actionable for staff after departure and across rehire. Add focused worklists, reminders, reasoned corrections, lost and waived outcomes, recovery, personal data exports, and configurable finite retention. Preserve the distinction between fulfilling a return obligation and making an item available to issue again.

69. As an equipment administrator, I want to preserve nonpersonal item identity and availability after personal history cleanup, so that retention does not release an item for reissue or free its identifier.

## Implementation Decisions

- **Domain boundary and integration:** Organization owns equipment types, item identities, assignments, handovers, officer grants, and equipment retention policy. Employee lifecycle continues to own employment periods, departure cutoff, deactivation, and access. Notifications owns channel delivery and preferences; the existing personal data export pipeline owns collection and archive delivery. Equipment adds narrow integrations to these boundaries rather than duplicating those systems.
- **Public equipment boundary:** Provide validated server actions and authorized read models for policy and officer administration, types and individually identified items, issuance, receipt, transfer, loss or waiver, recovery, correction, clearance, employee history, and the staff overview. Resolve the actor and active organization on the server. Make the equipment operations the shared entry point for UI and lifecycle-driven changes; do not expose raw record editing as a second route around the rules.
- **Tenant isolation and authorization:** Persist organization identity on equipment-owned records. Validate every referenced employee, team, type, item, assignment, grant, and handover in the active organization. Reuse existing organization access and ability helpers. Owners and admins manage all equipment and officer grants; explicit officer grants authorize equipment work within their scope. Managers have no implied equipment read or write access. Employees can read their own retained equipment records.
- **Officer scope and employment:** Whole-organization, selected-team, and selected-employee scopes use current grants. Former employee follow-up uses team membership captured when the employee left, rather than current-roster membership. Capture this before lifecycle team cleanup. End the departing officer's grant and do not restore it automatically on rehire. Keep old obligations linked to their original employee and departure or employment context so old follow-up cannot rewrite a new employment period's assignments.
- **Equipment types and identity:** Reusable types define individual or quantity tracking and default return requirement. Store the agreed requirement on each assignment so later default edits do not change it. Archive used types. A used type cannot change tracking mode; create a new type instead. An individually identified item has an organization equipment identifier and optional manufacturer serial number. Identifier equality ignores surrounding spaces and case, and the identity stays reserved after retirement and personal-history cleanup.
- **Custody and quantity integrity:** An identified item has at most one holder and cannot be issued while still held, lost, unavailable, retired, or awaiting required damaged-item clearance. Quantity issuance and settlement use positive whole quantities. A physical receipt, loss closure, and waiver are distinct outcomes; effective settlements cannot exceed the issued quantity. Partial receipts preserve their own dates, conditions, notes, and actors. An effective recovery of a quantity already closed as lost does not settle that same quantity twice.
- **Atomicity, retries, and concurrency:** Perform issuance, settlement, transfer, correction, recovery, and cleanup through transactions with database-enforced identity and custody constraints and a consistent lock order. A combined transfer commits receipt and new issuance together or neither. Request identity and expected revision protect retries and stale forms; replaying the same command must not issue or receive equipment twice. Serialize issuance with the relevant employee lifecycle state so a racing departure cannot create a newly issued assignment for a former employee. Recheck authorization and scope at execution, including queued follow-up.
- **Handovers, conditions, and chronology:** Use good, worn, damaged, or unknown condition with optional notes on every issuance and physical receipt. Record actual handover date separately from the UTC entry timestamp and actor. Historical issuance can explicitly have an unknown date; do not substitute the entry date or invent chronology. New handovers need their actual date. Reject future dates and conflicting histories, including an identified item with overlapping custody. Former employees permit historical completion of records, not fresh issuance.
- **Physical receipt and exceptions:** An authorized receiver confirms physical receipt. Only owners and admins close or reverse lost and waived outcomes, always with a reason. A waiver authorizes non-return but does not report receipt. Recovery records physical receipt and condition alongside the original loss history. Staff may record issuance to themselves, but another authorized receiver must confirm their return and another owner or admin must close their own obligation as lost or waived. Corrections cannot bypass these restrictions.
- **Availability and issued-to-keep equipment:** Equipment issued to keep has no return deadline, outstanding return, or reminders. An individually identified item issued to keep remains unavailable for another issuance until physically received back. Turning an existing required return into permission to keep uses the owner/admin waiver path. Receiving a damaged item fulfills the return obligation but leaves it unavailable pending explicit officer clearance. Clearance is a separate record and never overwrites the condition at receipt. Support unavailable and retired states without a repair system or warehouse balances.
- **Corrections and history:** Preserve original business records and add attributable, reasoned amendments. Validate the resulting effective history through the same quantity, custody, authorization, chronology, and self-confirmation rules as ordinary commands. Corrections cannot create a second holder or change an exception outcome through an officer-only path. Retained original records are subject to the agreed retention policy; history preservation is not permanent retention of personal information.
- **Return deadlines:** Keep an explicit return deadline separate from any departure-derived deadline and use the earlier applicable date. Required equipment without an explicit deadline need not be overdue during employment. Scheduled departure makes it due by the last working day unless already due earlier; immediate deactivation makes it due on that organization-local date unless earlier. Editing or cancelling a scheduled departure changes only its derived deadline, preserving explicit dates and recorded handovers. Outstanding means required and not settled; overdue means the effective deadline is before the organization's current calendar date.
- **Lifecycle integration:** Extend both departure preview/commands and deactivation preview/apply. Recompute on submit; a preview is advisory. Capture outstanding follow-up and officer scope before roster and team changes. Keep physical return follow-up independent of whether departure becomes effective, consistent with the existing lifecycle ADR. Rehire leaves old obligations and their existing deadlines intact and does not restore ended officer grants. Revoking kiosk credentials is independent of whether their hardware is received.
- **Time model:** Use Temporal for calendar dates, comparisons, and scheduling with explicit organization zones. Persist canonical audit instants in UTC. Viewer timezone must not change handover date meaning, deadlines, overdue filters, or reminder occasions. Respect the frozen zone and cutoff of a scheduled departure rather than recomputing its business date from a viewer's local time.
- **Reminders:** Add an equipment notification type using existing recipient-language, preference, and delivery infrastructure. Default to in-app and email, respecting user preferences. Employee occasions are three calendar days before and on the deadline. Responsible officers get the first overdue occasion and weekly follow-up thereafter; use owners/admins when there is no authorized officer. After employment access ends, notify currently authorized staff only. Derive recipients from current permissions, keep messages organization-scoped, and stop for resolved quantities. Use durable occasion identity to prevent duplicates on repeated worker runs and recalculate eligibility when deadlines or outcomes change.
- **Views and forms:** Provide an authorized Equipment overview, equipment on employee profiles, a personal equipment view, and outstanding-return summaries in departure and deactivation previews. Filter the overview by holder, type, outstanding, overdue, and former employee. Former employees must remain discoverable for follow-up without being offered for fresh issuance. Show returned, lost, waived, recovered, issued-to-keep, and outstanding information without implying they are interchangeable. Apply the same permissions to navigation, direct routes, reads, and writes. Use existing form, icon, localization, and light/dark UI conventions.
- **Personal data exports:** Register equipment as a category in the existing subject-and-organization-scoped collection pipeline, including field explanations and archive descriptions. Include the employee's retained assignments, conditions, notes, corrections, and exception outcomes in employee and admin exports. Do not introduce an equipment-only withheld-data category. Former employees continue through the admin-assisted export flow. Cleanup must be reflected in subsequent reads and exports.
- **Retention and holds:** Require a positive finite organization retention policy before operational use. For fully closed assignment records, count from their latest activity. Protect outstanding obligations and documented case holds. Cleanup removes employee links and personal details, including retained free text and identifying history, while preserving only nonpersonal item identity and availability. Purging an issued-to-keep or lost assignment's personal history must not make an item available or release its identifier. Coordinate cleanup with corrections, receipts, recoveries, and holds so it cannot purge newly active or protected records.
- **Delivery constraints:** Use the established database migration mechanism with monotonic migration ordering, the shared disposable-database integration harness, and the repository's existing runtime and authorization patterns. Do not edit generated authentication schema. Configuration belongs to the organization in the application, not tenant-specific environment variables. No new external service is needed for this feature.

## Testing Decisions

The user confirmed these testing boundaries before publication. They use the highest practical public entry points rather than tests for private helpers or table layout.

1. **Primary boundary: equipment public actions and authorized reads with real PostgreSQL.** Drive complete issue, receipt, partial return, exception, recovery, clearance, transfer, and correction workflows through the same boundary as the UI. Assert returned read models and observable persisted outcomes. Keep production database queries, transactions, and constraints real. Substitute only request/session context, clock, and external effects through the shared test harness. Prior art is the driving licence original-check, verifier-grant, history, and worklist integration suites, which invoke public actions and read employee-visible state.
2. **Reuse existing integration boundaries for cross-context effects.** Extend employee lifecycle command and deactivation acceptance tests for preview, deadline changes, cutoff, grant ending, access independence, and rehire. Invoke the reminder and retention workers through their existing public execution pattern with a deterministic Temporal clock and observable deliveries or cleanup outcomes. Exercise equipment inclusion through the existing personal data export processor and inspect generated archive content. Prior art is departure command and acceptance suites, deactivation access-ending suites, personnel document request reminder tests, driving licence retention tests, and personal data export archive integration tests.
3. **Focused UI behavior and runtime smoke checks.** Check that the authorized overview, employee profile section, own-equipment view, and departure/deactivation summaries open correctly and use their public actions. Cover accessible form errors, quantity entry and partial returns, condition display, former-employee filters, and permission-dependent navigation/actions. Reuse existing route-guard and component-testing conventions and the normal application runtime verification workflow. Avoid building a new broad browser-test framework for this spec or duplicating every server business rule in component tests.

Required behavioral coverage:

- Issue three required shirts; receive two good shirts and close one as lost with a reason. Read back two physically returned, one lost, and zero outstanding. Also cover separately received good and damaged quantities, zero/negative/fractional/excess settlements, and partial loss/waiver reversals.
- Two concurrent issuances of one identified item yield one holder. Concurrent partial returns or conflicting corrections cannot oversettle. Repeated command identity replays a committed result rather than creating a second event. A combined transfer cannot leave only one handover committed.
- Exercise owners/admins, whole-organization officers, team officers, employee-scoped officers, ordinary employees, managers without grants, departed officers, and an actor in another organization. Include forged foreign identifiers, direct action calls, direct-route access, revoked grants, and changes to team scope. Verify employees cannot read another employee's record or confirm their own return, and admins cannot waive their own obligation.
- Schedule, move, cancel, immediately execute, and deactivate departures. Explicit earlier deadlines survive; cancellation removes only the derived deadline. A missing laptop never keeps access or employment active. Former-employee returns stay actionable despite roster removal. Rehire neither clears old obligations nor restores officer grants. A racing issue and departure produces a valid serial outcome. Returning a key/fob never reactivates its credential.
- Verify historical entry with known and explicitly unknown issue dates, entry timestamps separate from handover dates, no future handovers, no overlapping identified-item custody, and no newly issued equipment for former employees.
- Test reminders at three days before, on deadline, first overdue day, and weekly staff follow-up; repeat worker runs; change deadlines; resolve quantities; revoke officer scope; and depart the employee. Check preference-aware in-app/email delivery, currently authorized staff and fallback, and no former-employee reminders. Include organization-local midnight and DST cases with a viewer or test process in another timezone.
- Receive a damaged laptop: outstanding becomes zero but a new issuance fails until separate clearance. Close an item as lost: issuance fails until recorded recovery. An issued-to-keep item has no deadline or reminders and cannot be reissued without receipt. Case/space-equivalent and retired identifiers cannot be reused. Type default changes leave existing assignments unchanged.
- Run retention before and after the configured boundary, after subsequent activity, with outstanding obligations, and under a case hold. Race cleanup with a correction or receipt. Verify expired personal details disappear from authorized reads and new exports while nonpersonal identifiers and availability remain correct, including issued-to-keep and lost equipment.
- Request self and admin-assisted personal data exports for current and former subjects in one organization, inspect equipment content and field explanations, and prove another organization's records are absent. After cleanup, exported content must not resurrect removed personal history.

Database suites belong to the existing PostgreSQL integration project and use its disposable runner; database-dependent tests must fail rather than silently skip when the disposable database is absent. Keep pure calendar tests only where they expose meaningful boundary behavior. Run targeted integration suites and relevant existing lifecycle/export regressions during implementation, plus the repository's required build/type/lint and UI checks. This spec-writing step does not claim those implementation tests have already run.

## Out of Scope

- Purchasing, depreciation, accounting asset valuation, warehouse balances, stock replenishment, repair workflows, and financial recovery or payroll deductions.
- Bulk imports, equipment photo uploads, signed handover documents, mandatory employee acknowledgment, and employee-to-employee transfers without authorized staff confirmation.
- Using physical equipment return as a gate for employment ending, paid-seat release, access revocation, or credential revocation.
- Granting equipment visibility or mutation authority merely because someone is a manager, or permitting self-confirmed returns and self-approved lost/waived outcomes.
- Replacing the employee lifecycle, notification delivery, personal data export, form, or authentication systems with feature-specific alternatives.

The central invariant is that return obligation, physical custody, readiness for another issue, employment/access, and personal-record retention are distinct. A damaged item may be returned but unavailable; a waived or lost item may have no outstanding obligation but still not be physically received; personal history may expire without releasing an item identity; employment ends independently of all of these.
