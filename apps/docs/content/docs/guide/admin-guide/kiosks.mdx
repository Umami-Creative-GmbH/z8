A kiosk is a shared device, such as a tablet at the staff entrance, where employees clock in and out with a kiosk PIN instead of signing in. Each kiosk belongs to one location and accepts only the employees assigned to that location.

---

## Setting Up a Kiosk

Organization owners and admins manage kiosks in **Settings -> Kiosks**. Create a location first: every kiosk belongs to one.

1. Choose **Add kiosk**.
2. Enter a **Name** (for example "Staff entrance"), choose the **Location**, and check the **Time zone**. Clock events recorded at the kiosk use this zone.
3. Optionally switch on the **Who-is-in board**, which shows on the kiosk who is in or on a break, by first name and last initial.
4. Choose **Create kiosk**. Z8 shows a pairing code and a QR code.
5. On the kiosk device, scan the QR code, or open the kiosk page and enter the code. The code works once and expires after 10 minutes.

The device stays paired until you pair it again or revoke the kiosk. Changes to a kiosk apply at its next request.

| Action | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Pair again** | Issues a new pairing code. If a device is paired, its token is rotated: that device stops working at once and must be paired again with the new code. For a kiosk with offline mode on, Z8 warns you first: clockings it saved offline and has not sent yet are refused when it reconnects. |
| **Revoke** | The kiosk stops working at its next request and cannot be paired again. Create a new kiosk to replace it. |

## Who Can Clock at a Kiosk

An employee can clock at a kiosk when they are:

- an active employee of the organization,
- assigned to the kiosk's location (on the employee or on the location, in **Settings**), and
- set up with a kiosk PIN of 4 to 6 digits.

Employees with an account choose their own kiosk PIN in their security settings. Managers can create kiosk-only employees, who have no email address, and set, reset or unlock their PIN on the employee's page in **Settings -> Employees**.

After five wrong PINs in a row the employee's PIN is locked for 15 minutes. A kiosk also accepts at most 30 PIN attempts per minute, whoever enters them.

## Kiosk Badges

A kiosk badge is a printed QR code that identifies an employee at a kiosk instead of their PIN. It never signs anyone in to Z8, and the employee's kiosk PIN keeps working alongside it. A kiosk reads badges only when its badge scanner or its badge camera is switched on (see [Clocking with a Badge](#clocking-with-a-badge)).

Owners, admins and the employee's direct manager manage badges in the **Kiosk access** card on the employee's page in **Settings -> Employees**, one employee at a time. Only active employees, including kiosk-only employees, can receive a badge.

| Action | Effect |
|--------|--------|
| **Issue badge** | Creates the employee's badge and offers it as a PDF with their name, the organization and the QR code. An employee has at most one active badge, and it does not expire. |
| **Replace badge** | Creates a new badge and PDF. Every copy of the old badge stops working at once. |
| **Revoke badge** | Every copy of the badge stops working for good. The employee's kiosk PIN is not changed. |

<Callout type="warn" title="Save the PDF when you issue the badge">
Z8 keeps only a fingerprint of the badge, never the badge itself, so it cannot show or download the same badge again. Save the PDF (**Download PDF**) or print it (**Open to print**) before you choose **Done** or leave the page. You can reprint a saved PDF as often as you need. If the file or a printed badge is lost, replace the badge.
</Callout>

Whoever holds a copy of a badge can use it, so treat it like a key. Cut it out along the dashed line; it fits a standard card sleeve.

### Badges and Departures

A badge ends with the employee's departure. It keeps working until the departure's cutoff, the start of the day after the last working day in the organization's timezone, and stops working at that moment, even if Z8 has not finished processing the departure yet. Scheduling a departure does not end the badge early; if you move or cancel the departure before its cutoff, the badge follows the current date. The card then shows that the last badge ended with the departure.

A rehire never brings an old badge back: every copy of it stays invalid. To let a rehired employee use a badge again, issue a new one. Until the rehire, Z8 offers no new badge. The employee's kiosk PIN is not changed by a departure or a rehire.

## Kiosk Fobs

A kiosk fob is an NFC card or key fob that an employee taps on a reader attached to the kiosk. The fob only **selects** the employee, in place of tapping their name. The employee still enters their kiosk PIN, which proves who they are. A fob never signs anyone in to Z8, and it never clocks anyone without the PIN.

Z8 uses only the chip's serial number, the number every 13.56 MHz card has. Nothing is written to the card, so any such card works, including an access card employees already carry. A serial number is no secret, which is why the PIN is still needed.

A kiosk reads fobs only when its **NFC fob reader** switch is on (see [Setting Up the Fob Reader](#setting-up-the-fob-reader)).

### Registering a Fob

Employees register their own fob at a kiosk with their kiosk PIN. They don't need an admin:

1. The employee taps the new fob on the kiosk's reader. The kiosk says **This fob is not recognised here** and offers **Register this fob**.
2. They choose **Register this fob**, tap their name, and enter their kiosk PIN.
3. The kiosk confirms the registration and shows only the last four characters of the serial number.

Things to know:

- **One fob per employee.** Registering a new fob replaces the employee's earlier one at once. The kiosk says so.
- **A fob belongs to one employee.** A fob that is already registered to another employee in the organization is refused. The kiosk doesn't say who it belongs to, and the fob is never moved silently. Another organization can register the same card for its own employee.
- **The PIN rules apply.** A wrong PIN registers nothing and counts toward the employee's PIN lockout, as when clocking. Only employees who can clock at this kiosk can register a fob here.
- **The serial number stays hidden.** Z8 and the kiosk only ever show its last four characters.

## Clocking at a Kiosk

1. The employee taps their name on the kiosk's home screen, or taps their kiosk fob on the reader (see [Clocking with a Fob](#clocking-with-a-fob)).
2. They enter their kiosk PIN.
3. The kiosk shows their state and today's day total, and only the actions that fit: **Clock in**, or **Start break** and **Clock out**, or **Resume work** and **End day**. Ending the day while on a break ends the work at the start of the break.
4. On **Clock out**, the kiosk asks which project and work category the work was for (see [Projects and Work Categories at Clock-Out](#projects-and-work-categories-at-clock-out)).
5. When vehicle use is asked or expected for the employee, the kiosk asks for the vehicle and odometer reading before the action (see [Vehicles and Kilometres](/docs/guide/admin-guide/vehicles-and-kilometres)).
6. A confirmation shows the action and its time, then the kiosk returns to the home screen.

The kiosk keeps an employee's PIN, state, day total and project choices only for their turn. It does not show history or corrections.

### Projects and Work Categories at Clock-Out

When an employee clocks out while connected, the kiosk shows the project and work category the work is booked to. The employee can keep them (the default), choose another one, or choose **No project** or **No work category**. The choice applies only to the work that ends now, not to earlier work that day.

- The kiosk offers the same choices as the web clock: projects the employee may book (assigned to them or their team, in a bookable status, while projects are on for the organization), and the active work categories of their category set. Kiosk-only employees get their own choices.
- Nothing is required. An employee with nothing to choose clocks out at once, and keeping a project or category that is no longer offered still works.
- Z8 checks the choice again when the work ends. If the project or category can no longer be booked, nothing is recorded; the kiosk says so and keeps the choice, so the employee can choose again or keep the current one.
- The kiosk asks for no task and no billability. Changing the project follows the usual rules: the task is cleared and the project's billable default applies. A project that requires a task is booked without one, and the work is marked as missing a task.
- Clocking in, breaks, **End day** on a break and offline clocking ask nothing and keep the work's project and category.

### Clocking with a Badge

A kiosk reads kiosk badges in two ways, which owners and admins switch on separately per kiosk in the kiosk's **Edit** dialog. Both are off for every kiosk until you switch them on, and switching one off never changes the other. The kiosks list shows "Badge scanner on" and "Badge camera on" for the kiosks that use them.

- **Badge scanner**: a QR scanner attached to the device, the kind that types what it reads like a keyboard (often called a keyboard or "wedge" scanner, connected by USB or Bluetooth). Set the scanner up as a keyboard that ends each scan with Enter (most scanners do by default). Badges use only digits and capital letters without Y and Z, so they read correctly whatever keyboard layout the scanner or the device uses.
- **Badge camera**: the kiosk device's own camera. The home screen then shows **Scan badge** (see [Scanning with the Kiosk's Camera](#scanning-with-the-kiosks-camera)).

With either input on:

1. The employee scans their badge at the kiosk's home screen: at the scanner, or by tapping **Scan badge** and holding the badge up to the camera. They do not tap their name or enter a PIN, and the scan alone records nothing.
2. The kiosk shows their state, today's day total and the actions that fit, as after a PIN.
3. They choose the action; vehicle questions and the confirmation follow as usual, and the kiosk returns to the home screen.

Things to know:

- **Only the home screen reads badges.** A scan during someone's turn, on a confirmation or while the kiosk checks an action is ignored: it cannot switch the employee or press anything. Typing a name into the search works as before.
- **Every action checks the badge again.** If the badge was replaced or revoked, the employee's departure took effect, they are no longer assigned to the kiosk's location, the kiosk was revoked or the input that read the badge (scanner or camera) was switched off since the scan, the action is refused and nothing is recorded.
- **Refused badges name nobody.** An unknown, replaced, revoked or foreign badge, or one of an employee who cannot clock at this kiosk, shows the same message. A code that is not a kiosk badge (a link, a product barcode) is never opened.
- **Badge clocking needs a connection.** Offline, the kiosk says so and records and saves nothing for the badge. The employee can tap their name and use their PIN; with offline mode on, that PIN clocking is saved on the kiosk as usual.
- **PIN and badge are independent.** Wrong badges never count toward an employee's PIN lockout. A kiosk accepts at most 60 badge requests per minute (a scan and its action are two).
- **A lost answer is checked, never repeated.** If the connection drops after the employee chose an action, the kiosk shows "Checking whether your action was recorded" and keeps checking in the background, also after a reload. Only that employee waits: **Back** (or 30 seconds without a touch) returns the kiosk to everyone else, and offline PIN clocking keeps working for them. Until Z8 answers, the waiting employee gets no PIN pad and no actions when they tap their name or scan their badge, only the same "Checking" screen. If the action was recorded, the kiosk shows its original time once, when they come back. If Z8 confirms nothing was recorded, the kiosk tells them so and lets them choose the action again; it never sends the old action on its own.

### Scanning with the Kiosk's Camera

With **Badge camera** on, the kiosk's home screen shows **Scan badge**. The camera turns on only when an employee taps it:

1. The kiosk asks the browser for the camera. The first time, the browser asks whether the page may use it: allow it once on the kiosk device. Z8 lets only the kiosk page use the camera; the microphone stays blocked.
2. The kiosk shows the camera picture. The employee holds their badge's QR code inside the frame. The front camera, which faces the person at a tablet on a stand, is used when there is one.
3. As soon as the kiosk reads the badge, the camera turns off and the employee's turn starts as with the scanner.

The camera turns off when it has read a badge, when the employee taps **Cancel scanning**, after 30 seconds without a touch, when the kiosk page is hidden or closed, and when you switch **Badge camera** off. It stays off during the employee's turn, on the confirmation and while the kiosk checks an action. Holding up the badge again, or another badge, during a turn changes nothing.

The QR code is read on the kiosk device. No picture is uploaded, stored or attached to any work: Z8 receives only the badge's code, as from the scanner. There is no photo capture and no face recognition.

When the camera cannot scan, the kiosk says why on the home screen, and the employee taps their name and uses their PIN instead:

| Message | What to do |
|---------|------------|
| The kiosk may not use the camera | Camera access was refused. Allow the camera for the kiosk page in the browser's site settings, then reload the page. |
| This kiosk has no camera that can scan | Connect a camera, or use a badge scanner instead. |
| This browser cannot scan badges with the camera | Use a current version of Chrome, Edge, Safari or Firefox. The kiosk must be opened over HTTPS. |
| The camera could not be started | Another app may be using the camera. Close it and try again. |

A code the camera reads that is not a kiosk badge is never opened: the kiosk says so and keeps looking. Badge clocking with the camera needs a connection, like the scanner; offline, **Scan badge** says so and does not turn the camera on.

<Callout type="info" title="Printed or on screen">
The camera reads the badge printed from its PDF. Print it at full size on white paper and keep it flat and free of glare. A badge PDF shown on another screen also works, but treat that screen like the badge itself.
</Callout>

### Clocking with a Fob

At a kiosk with the **NFC fob reader** on, the home screen says **Or tap your fob on the reader.**

1. The employee taps their registered fob on the reader at the kiosk's home screen. The tap alone records nothing.
2. The kiosk opens their PIN entry, as if they had tapped their name.
3. From here everything works as in [Clocking at a Kiosk](#clocking-at-a-kiosk): they enter their kiosk PIN, and the kiosk shows their state, today's day total and the actions that fit.

Things to know:

- **The PIN always proves.** A wrong PIN counts toward the same lockout as with name and PIN. After five wrong PINs the employee's PIN is locked for 15 minutes, whether they started with their name or their fob.
- **Only the home screen reads fobs.** A tap during someone's turn, on a confirmation or while the kiosk checks an action is ignored. It can't switch the employee, type digits into the PIN pad or press anything.
- **Fobs that can't be used here name nobody.** An unknown or replaced fob gets the same **This fob is not recognised here** answer as the fob of an employee who can't clock at this kiosk. That includes an employee at another location, an inactive or departed employee, and one from another organization. The kiosk offers **Register this fob** either way. Registering is refused if the fob belongs to someone else.
- **Fob clocking needs a connection.** While offline, the kiosk says that fobs need a connection and sends or saves nothing for the fob. With offline mode on, the employee can tap their name and clock with their PIN as usual.
- **An unresolved action holds the employee.** While the kiosk checks whether an employee's last action was recorded, their fob opens the same "Checking" screen as tapping their name, with no PIN pad.
- A kiosk accepts at most 60 fob taps per minute. Registrations count toward its 30 PIN attempts per minute.

### Setting Up the Fob Reader

Owners and admins switch on **NFC fob reader** per kiosk in the kiosk's **Edit** dialog. It is off for every kiosk until you switch it on, and it's independent of the badge scanner and badge camera. The kiosks list shows "NFC fob reader on" for the kiosks that use it. Switching it off stops fob taps and registrations at that kiosk at once. Registered fobs are kept and work again when you switch it back on.

The reader must work like a keyboard. It types the serial number when a card is tapped, the way a badge scanner types a badge. Z8 accepts the serial number only in **hexadecimal** (digits 0–9 and letters A–F), 8, 14 or 20 characters long (4-, 7- or 10-byte chips). Upper or lower case, separators (`:`, `-`, spaces) and a final Enter are all fine. Don't use decimal output: most decimal serial numbers are ignored, and the rest wouldn't match the fob's registration.

**Reference reader: ACS ACR1552U.** Set it up once, with the configuration tool ACS provides for the reader:

1. Switch the reader to **keyboard mode** (keyboard emulation), so it types each card's serial number.
2. Set the output format to **hexadecimal**. Leave the byte order as the reader reads it: Z8 never swaps bytes, so a reversed serial number is a different fob.
3. Optionally end each read with **Enter**.
4. Connect the reader to the kiosk device by USB. Open a text editor on the device and tap a card: a line like `04A1B2C3D4E5F6` should appear.

Hexadecimal uses only digits and the letters A–F, so serial numbers read correctly whatever keyboard layout the device uses. If you change a reader's output format after employees have registered fobs, their fobs no longer match. Set every reader in the organization up the same way.

<Callout type="info" title="Fob or badge?">
Both start a turn at the home screen, and both can be on at the same kiosk. A badge stands in for the PIN. A fob only replaces tapping the name, so the PIN is still needed. A fob's serial number can be read or copied without the employee noticing, so Z8 never treats it as proof.
</Callout>

## Offline Mode

<Callout type="info" title="Not Yet Available">
  Offline mode is not available yet. It needs your organization to use coordinated clock commands, which no organization does yet. Until then, a kiosk works only while it is online, and a kiosk without a connection says so and records nothing.
</Callout>

With offline mode, a kiosk keeps clocking when its connection drops, also after a reload or a restart of the device. Owners and admins switch it on per kiosk with **Offline mode** in the kiosk's **Edit** dialog; it is off by default.

### What employees see offline

- The kiosk shows a banner that it is offline and that clockings are saved on the kiosk.
- After the employee taps their name and enters their PIN, the kiosk shows their state **as of** the time it last heard from Z8, together with what was already saved on this kiosk since. It never shows a day total offline.
- All actions work as online, including ending the day while on a break and recording vehicle use.
- The confirmation says the clocking is **saved on this kiosk and not yet confirmed**.

### How offline clockings are confirmed

The kiosk cannot check a PIN offline, and it keeps nothing that could: it seals each entered PIN so that only Z8 can read it. When the connection is back, the kiosk sends its saved clockings in the order they happened. Z8 then checks each PIN against the employee's current kiosk PIN, applies the PIN lockout in the order the attempts happened, and records each clocking at the time it happened. Each clocking is recorded once, even when the kiosk has to send it again.

The kiosk applies its limit of 30 PIN attempts per minute itself while offline. If five saved PINs of the same employee turn out wrong within 15 minutes of arriving, Z8 checks that employee's further saved clockings only after those 15 minutes. The kiosk keeps them and sends them again, so nothing is lost.

### Time of offline clockings

The kiosk takes the time from Z8's clock at its last contact and counts on with a clock the device user cannot change. After a restart without a connection, or after the device was asleep, it uses the device clock instead, and the entry records which time source was used. Clockings are recorded in the time zone the kiosk had when the employee clocked. Z8 accepts offline clockings up to 7 days late.

### Refused offline clockings

Z8 refuses a saved clocking it cannot accept, for example a wrong or locked PIN, an employee no longer assigned to the location, or a time outside the accepted window. If a kiosk is revoked or paired again while offline, Z8 refuses all of its saved clockings.

For each refused clocking:

- the kiosk shows a short notice on its home screen;
- the employee gets a notification saying what was not recorded and why. For a kiosk-only employee, their managers get it instead;
- owners and admins see it under **Refused offline clockings** in **Settings -> Kiosks**, with the employee, the action, the time it claimed, the time source and the reason.

### What the kiosk keeps

An offline-mode kiosk keeps the kiosk's details, the assigned employees with their last known state and their vehicle-use details, and its saved clockings with their sealed PINs. It deletes what it no longer needs at its next contact: everything when offline mode is switched off or the kiosk is revoked, and an employee once they are no longer assigned to the location. Saved clockings are still sent and judged.
