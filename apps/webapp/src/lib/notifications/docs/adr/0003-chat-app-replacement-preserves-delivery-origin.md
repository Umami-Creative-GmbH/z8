# Chat app replacement preserves delivery origin

Organizations own the credentials for new Slack, Microsoft Teams, Telegram, and Discord connections. Z8 can verify a replacement while the current connection continues to serve employees. Each previously sent approval card retains its originating installation so a switch can move new delivery without changing who can act on an existing card.

## Ownership and migration

- An organization has one active installation per provider after switching. A Slack workspace or Microsoft Teams tenant belongs to one Z8 organization. That organization can prepare, run, and retire separate apps for the same workspace or tenant. Enforce the active installation limit in the database. Activation compares the reviewed current installation and candidate credential revisions before committing.
- Store workspace, tenant, and bot ownership claims separately from installation rows. A unique provider and external identity identifies the owning organization. Each installation references the matching claim and organization. The same organization can hold several installations against its claim. Use workspace-scoped Slack app identity, Teams application ID, Telegram numeric bot ID, and Discord application ID. Acquire a bot claim before mutating its provider endpoint.
- Keep existing provider rows and uniqueness constraints during the additive rollout. Link them as legacy provenance and backfill ownership claims from verified external identities. Detect conflicting legacy bot identities before assigning a claim. A duplicate or unknown identity appears as a remediation state, and blocks the affected switch and endpoint mutations until resolved. Keep legacy traffic on its existing routes during remediation. A replacement lives in the new installation store from the start. The new organization and provider settings record becomes authoritative at switch. Before then, legacy rows retain their effective settings and traffic. When legacy Teams settings disagree, show the differences and ask the admin to confirm the settings for the replacement during setup.
- Preserve every live legacy Teams tenant until the first organization-wide switch. The Teams tab lists each discovered tenant, its status, and any remediation needed. Keep unfamiliar legacy statuses visible for remediation. Each tenant has a separately scoped revoke action. The impact review lists the full set of live tenants and settings being replaced. One transaction activates the verified app and retires all reviewed live legacy tenants. Each retiring tenant can still service its own cards. An unexpected change to the reviewed set requires a fresh review.
- Preserve historical receiver scopes. Record the installation on new canonical and legacy delivery attempts and messages. Resolve historical messages through evidenced organization, provider, receiver scope, and legacy installation. An ambiguous origin goes to the Z8 inbox. Approval delivery controls retain their organization, workflow, and provider scope. [Approval delivery reference](../../../../../../../docs/refs/approval-delivery.md)

## Settings and setup

- Keep the four existing provider tabs and deep links at `/settings/notification-channels`. Stack current, replacement, and retiring connections on narrow screens. Show external identity, credential source, last verification, and an actionable recovery state. Distinguish load failure from disconnection. Give mounted tabs unique field IDs, announce verification changes, and restore focus when dialogs close.
- Separate **Prepare**, **Test**, and **Switch**. Test uses a synthetic private message and provider-verified inbound proof. It cannot send production notifications or act on approvals. Show feature prerequisites separately from connection readiness. The switch review shows reachable employees, employees who need to act, unknowns, outstanding cards, and in-flight work. The admin can proceed with unready employees after acknowledging the impact. If the impact materially changes, announce the change, focus the impact summary, and require the admin to confirm the revised impact.
- Slack setup uses a generated manifest, a bot token, and a signing secret. Verify the app and workspace by checking the token's bot identity and bot metadata, then require a signed app and workspace event and a private test message. Teams setup supplies a single-tenant app package and Microsoft admin rollout instructions. Confirm an authenticated activity for the configured app and tenant, then verify a proactive private reply. Telegram verifies bot ownership, authenticated webhook receipt, and command registration independently. Discord verifies the application and signed interaction endpoint, reports command registration separately, and checks private delivery.
- Members manage their chat links under **Settings → Notifications**. Use one organization-scoped availability read for the settings screen and the preferences API. Update bot help to lead members there. Reuse an exact verified account binding only within its original scope and verify reachability through the replacement. In Teams, a member with a proven organization, tenant, and Entra object ID binding can open a private conversation with the new bot. Other members link through a short-lived code created in Z8 and redeemed in a private conversation with that installation. Use the same purpose-bound, single-use proof model for the other providers. Store code digests, rate-limit attempts, check current member eligibility at redemption, and consume the proof atomically. Keep candidate enrollment evidence separate from current delivery and account links. Telegram members must contact the new bot. Discord reachability requires a delivery check. Account-wide preferences follow [ADR 0002](0002-notification-preferences-are-user-level.md).
- Derive the displayed approval capabilities from existing delivery and pilot admission rules, including authority and workflow type. The existing baseline includes canonical absence actions on Teams, Telegram, and Discord when admitted, and review in Z8 on Slack. The pilot admits Telegram for legacy absence and time actions and legacy travel expenses. For canonical time kinds the pilot admits Telegram actions and Slack review links. Label unverified combinations as such. When an approval policy owns escalation timing, explain that the channel setting controls delivery. ## Credentials and provider endpoints

- Use organization-scoped, immutable credential revisions with the existing Vault and Scaleway stores. Treat a confirmed missing revision and a store outage as distinct. An unavailable secret store blocks credential use and keeps cleanup retryable. Verify the new revision before committing it. A same-identity rotation preserves installation and conversation identity. Show any interruption the provider may impose, and require fresh verification before reactivation.
- Select the verifier with the installation's route identifier, authenticate the provider, and match external identity inside the authenticated context. Keep route identifiers separate from secrets. Scope adapter caches to installation and revision. Recheck current organization-admin access at setup, callback, credential, switch, and revoke operations. Restrict legacy inbound routes to the legacy installations they can authenticate. Retire old setup mutation paths, including Slack's deployment OAuth start and callback, or have still-supported routes call the same authorized lifecycle service. Teams resolves links through verified membership and external identity instead of email matching.
- Serialize endpoint registration and removal by stable bot identity. Persist the generation that authorized each cleanup operation and recheck it under the same serialization before each provider call or retry. A stale job skips a reactivated bot. A timed-out provider operation leaves endpoint state uncertain. Re-read and verify the provider state before reactivation, then reconcile the endpoint to the current generation before declaring it ready. Remove secret revisions only when nothing references them, retry partial cleanup, and cover recursive organization deletion in both stores. Record redacted lifecycle audit events.

## Delivery and retirement

- New sends resolve the active installation. Persist installation, revision, and dispatch intent before a durable provider call. A worker can reroute a confirmed unsent attempt under existing deduplication. A possibly accepted attempt, including a worker that died after dispatch, stays attached to its attempted installation. Across a switch, unresolved attempts go to operator attention and do not qualify for destination repair or automatic resend. After seven days they close with an explicit unknown outcome and inbox fallback. Surface actionable connection health to admins and route exhausted cleanup retries and unresolved delivery cases to platform operations through existing server error reporting.
- Apply the durable rule to approval delivery, persistent notification retries, and the Telegram digest ledger. Best-effort notifications and the other scheduled digests use the active installation for new work without replaying a prior run. Resume eligible destination-repair work only after the replacement destination is verified.
- Count old cards from the existing approval authority. Use current canonical delivery plus outstanding refresh work for that origin. For legacy cards, inspect whether the underlying request is still pending. A saved sent state alone does not establish an open approval. Retiring installations service those cards under current authorization and decision checks. Other actions use the existing Z8 destination when available. Retire when no eligible old cards or work remain or after 30 days. Immediate revocation ends local authorization. Approvals still pending remain in the Z8 inbox, and replacement activation does not reissue already-sent cards.

## Release evidence

Land the additive data and read paths before activating any organization-owned replacement. Exercise versions running side by side with continuous legacy functionality and block old mutation paths before enabling switches. Enable switches by provider and organization after the provider's tests pass. Rehearse duplicate and unknown legacy bot identities, a two-tenant legacy Teams switch, concurrent switch and dispatch, a provider-accepted send followed by worker death, old and new card races, changed employee or admin authority, and rollback. Inject secret-store outages and a delayed Telegram cleanup after reactivation. Verify isolation across organizations, candidate link proofs, expiry, and cleanup under both secret stores. Prove signed inbound traffic, private delivery, employee linking, rotation, and retiring-card behavior in real provider sandboxes. Exercise mobile layouts, keyboard and screen reader navigation, and English and German copy. These are release gates for the implementation.
